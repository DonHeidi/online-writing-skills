The discovery skills build the durable context the rest of the collection depends on. They are
**interviews, not forms** — the agent asks questions, reflects back drafts, and iterates until the
result sounds like you. Run them first; see [Getting Started](../../guides/getting-started/).

## `discover-purpose`

Define why you write online, who it's for, and what decisions future content should pass.

Use it when you can't articulate in one sentence why you write, who your audience is, or what you
want to be known for — or when purpose-level confusion is blocking writing decisions. The interview
moves through four phases:

1. **The Opening** — start broad and in life terms to surface the underlying motivation.
2. **Following the Thread** — explore the dimensions that are most alive: motivation, audience,
   category, point of view, style (educating vs. entertaining), and vision.
3. **Surfacing the Purpose** — reflect back a draft Purpose Statement synthesizing motivation →
   audience → category → POV → vision.
4. **Sharpening** — iterate until it feels like your own, not the agent's.

**Produces** `purpose.md`: the labeled dimensions, a natural-language Purpose Statement, and a
five-question Decision Filter for evaluating future writing choices.

## `discover-buckets`

Map your expertise into content buckets and topic territory.

Use it when you have a sense of purpose but don't know which topics you own, or you're stuck between
too many possible topics. The interview moves through five steps:

1. **Map expert zones** — list 10–20 things you know a lot about (breadth first).
2. **Find the One Big Key Zone** — the center of gravity everything connects back to.
3. **Find the Genius Zone** — overlap the key zone with 2–3 secondary zones for the unique
   intersection where you have an unfair advantage.
4. **Translate to content buckets** — General / Niche / Industry.
5. **Stress-test** — alignment, sustainability, specificity, energy.

**Produces** `expertise.md` (the full zone list) and `buckets.md` (the buckets and territory).

## `discover-tonality`

Build an English voice profile from examples and preferences.

Use it when output sounds generic, AI-generated, or misaligned with your natural voice — or when the
register mismatches the piece type (a case study reading as an opinion piece, a build log drilling a
thesis). It weaves preference questions, comparisons, and rewrite prompts across these stages:

1. **Setup** — start fresh or refine an existing profile; load `purpose.md` / `buckets.md` so examples
   come from your actual domain.
2. **Interview** — extract per-user values across six voice dimensions: Commitment, Reasoning Style,
   Reader Relationship, Emotional Register, Density, and agent-specific failure modes.
3. **Register check** — how the voice shifts by piece type (descriptive, argumentative, instructional,
   referential).
4. **Synthesis** — present a draft profile and iterate until you confirm it sounds like you.

**Produces** `tonality.md`: a voice summary, dimension profiles, agent-specific failure modes, register
tendencies, format rules, and 8–10 reference samples from your own rewrites. It's drafting guidance
the content skills load, not a style guide for human editing.

Use [`finde-stil`](../german/) instead — it produces `stil.md` from scratch in German and does
not read `tonality.md`.
:::
